About GSTAM

A scientific home for Malaysia's environmental practitioners.

GSTAM is a professional association of scientists, engineers, educators and policy practitioners working on the environmental challenges facing Malaysia — from renewable energy deployment to biodiversity loss. We exist to connect research with the people who can act on it.

Mission

Translate environmental science into action Malaysia can use.

We generate, curate and share applied research across green technology and environmental science — and put it directly into the hands of agencies, industry and communities who can put it to work.

Vision

A Malaysia where environmental science shapes everyday decisions.

From land use to industrial policy, we want scientific evidence to be the default starting point — not an afterthought — in how Malaysia builds its economy and manages its natural resources.

Objectives

What we commit to doing, in practice.

  • Advance research in renewable energy, biotechnology and the circular economy relevant to Malaysian conditions.
  • Support technical training and capacity-building for students, practitioners and public officers.
  • Provide independent scientific input to environmental policy discussions.
  • Facilitate collaboration between universities, government agencies, industry and civil society.
  • Communicate environmental science clearly to the public.

History

From a small working group of environmental researchers to a national association.

2011

A working group of environmental scientists begins meeting informally in Kuala Lumpur.

Researchers from several universities start sharing unpublished field data on forest degradation and water quality — the seed of what becomes GSTAM.

2014

GSTAM is formally registered as an association.

A founding council is elected and the association adopts its first constitution, focused on research dissemination and public education.

2017

First national symposium on green technology.

GSTAM convenes its first multi-institution symposium, bringing renewable energy and biotechnology researchers into direct dialogue with policymakers.

2019

Field programmes expand into mangrove and river-basin monitoring.

Long-term ecological monitoring partnerships begin in Perak and Sabah, forming the basis of GSTAM's current project portfolio.

2023

Training programmes cross 3,000 participants.

Cumulative reach of GSTAM's technical workshops and school outreach passes a significant milestone.

2026

GSTAM today.

Active across renewable energy, circular economy, biotechnology, ecosystem science and environmental education, with field partners across Peninsular and East Malaysia.

Organisation

GSTAM is governed by an elected council, with specialist committees leading each focus area.

President

Sets strategic direction and represents GSTAM to government and international partners.

Deputy President

Oversees programme delivery across all focus-area committees.

Honorary Secretary

Manages governance, membership and association records.

Honorary Treasurer

Oversees finances, grants and funding compliance.

Chair, Research Committee

Coordinates publications, peer review and research partnerships.

Chair, Education Committee

Leads training programmes, school outreach and public communication.

Council members are elected biennially by the GSTAM membership. Full governance documents are available to members on request.

Working together

Science is only useful when it's shared.

Every GSTAM programme is built jointly with the institutions and communities it serves — from field data collection with local fisherfolk to co-authored technical guidance for agencies.

Partner network

GSTAM works across four kinds of partners to move research into practice.

Research

Public and private universities collaborating on field studies and publications

Government

Federal and state agencies on forestry, energy and environmental policy

Industry

Renewable energy, waste management and manufacturing partners

Community

NGOs, Orang Asli communities and local fisherfolk associations
